## 1. Positioning

**Positioning statement**

> **KLARDOSIS is the German supplement brand for health-conscious adults who no longer know which products they can trust: every product is dosed below the BfR maximum levels by design, every batch's lab certificate is published online, and we use only EU-authorised claims. Where others sell "more", we sell "the right amount, proven".**

**Why this position, based on the report**
- **There is a clear, documented problem.** Öko-Test 06/2026 found half of 28 magnesium products above the BfR recommendation of 250 mg/day. Stiftung Warentest recommended only 2 of 25 vitamin D products and called all 18 kids' supplements tested "superfluous" [R§3]. Media coverage is regular and critical.
- **Consumers cannot judge quality themselves.** 49% wrongly believe products are safety-tested before sale, and 41% wrongly believe maximum levels are legally binding [R§3]. A brand that makes quality checkable fills this gap.
- **The position is open.** The report rates "science-dosed and transparent" as a position that no major player clearly owns (marked as an estimate) [R§4, Opportunity 1]. Natural Elements is positioned on high doses, which clashes with the overdosing debate. Sunday Natural owns "pure/additive-free" but not "correct dose". Orthomol owns pharmacist trust, but its magnesium was also rated "unnecessary" by Öko-Test [R§2].
- **The position is future-proof.** EU binding maximum levels are expected around 2028 [R§1, §4]. Formulating below BfR values today should reduce the risk of forced reformulation later. Competitors with high doses carry that risk.

**What KLARDOSIS is not**
- Not a price brand. The report states that competing with dm/Rossmann private labels on price is "hopeless" [R§2].
- Not a hype or influencer-claims brand like More Nutrition, which lost health-claims cases [R§2].
- Not a pharmacy brand at launch. The pharmacy channel is high-cost and needs a field force [R§4].

## 2. Target Segment

The report describes four segments [R§3]. We choose **two**, with a clear order.

### Primary segment: Clean-label and conscious buyers (report segment 4)
- **Who:** buys D2C online; wants additive-free, vegan, sustainable, transparent products [R§3].
- **Why first:**
  1. **They already buy in our planned launch channel (D2C).** Sunday Natural shows this segment pays mid-high prices online, and CVC valued it at about EUR 800 m [R§1, §2].
  2. **Transparency is their main purchase criterion.** Batch certificates and "no sweeteners, minimal additives" answer their needs directly. Öko-Test criticises sweeteners in 8 of 28 magnesium products [R§3].
  3. **They are willing to pay a premium.** That is the only way to escape the private-label price floor [R§2, §4].
- **Gap:** the report has **no data on the size** of this segment or on its willingness to pay a specific price, and no quantified German sustainability survey [R§5, data gaps]. **Insufficient information, further research recommended** (for example a short consumer survey or a price test before launch).

### Secondary segment: Health-conscious mainstream, mainly women 30–60 (report segment 1)
- **Who:** buys basics such as magnesium, vitamin D and immune products at dm/Rossmann; values price and convenience [R§3]. Women use supplements more than men, and 77% of respondents use supplements regularly [R§3].
- **Why second, not first:**
  - **For:** it is the largest segment, and it buys exactly the products where the test scandals happened (magnesium, vitamin D) [R§3]. Overdosing worries reach this group through mainstream media.
  - **Against:** it is price-sensitive and shops mainly in drugstores, where private labels dominate and listing is hard [R§4]. We only win them once we are listed at dm/Rossmann, which needs proof of demand first (see GTM Phase 3).
- **Product angle for this group:** needs-based bundles for women's health (cycle, menopause) as D2C subscriptions [R§4, Opportunity 4].

### Segments we do not target at launch
- **Ageing and needs-specific 50+ (segment 2):** they shop in pharmacies and want advice [R§3]. Pharmacy entry needs a wholesaler, a PZN and a field force: high cost [R§4]. Later option only.
- **Fitness Gen Z / Millennials (segment 3):** fastest-growing, but dominated by The Quality Group with EUR 1 bn+ revenue and an influencer engine [R§1, §2]. The report sees an opening for a "clean, legally solid" alternative [R§4, Opportunity 3]. We treat this as a **Phase 4 extension** (for example creatine with Kölner Liste testing), not as a launch segment, to avoid fighting TQG on its strongest ground with an unproven brand.

## 3. Differentiation

### Core differentiators ("the KLARDOSIS standard")
1. **Dosed below the BfR maximums by design.** Example: magnesium at or below 250 mg/day from the supplement [R§4]. This is shown as a fixed "Dosed within BfR guidance" mark on every pack.
2. **Batch lab certificates published online.** Each pack has a batch number / QR code linking to the lab report (heavy metals, contaminants) [R§4, Opportunity 1].
3. **Claims only from the EU Union list (Reg. 432/2012), worded close to the list.** No disease claims, no unapproved botanical claims, no Novel Food ingredients without approval [R§4]. This includes all influencer content, which counts as advertising.
4. **Clean formulation.** No sweeteners, minimal additives, vegan where possible [R§3, R§4 Opportunity 5].
5. **Honest guidance.** Product pages explain who does *not* need the product. This follows from Öko-Test calling magnesium "unnecessary" for healthy adults [R§2] and builds trust with a sceptical audience [R§3]. Whether this lowers conversion is unknown: **insufficient information, further research recommended (A/B test).**

A focused, small range instead of a very broad one. | **Natural Elements** | Value, high-dose, Amazon | High doses clash with the overdosing debate (estimate). We take the opposite stance: "right dose, not high dose". |

### Initial product range (focused)
Chosen because these are the most-used or fastest-growing categories in the report:
1. **Magnesium** (largest single micronutrient, EUR 119 m; centre of the Öko-Test overdosing finding) [R§1, §3].
2. **Vitamin D** (among the most used; only 2 of 25 products recommended by Stiftung Warentest) [R§3].
3. **Cultures-and-botanicals formula** (stomach and digestion EUR 419 m, now larger than vitamins and minerals) [R§1]. Note: probiotic and botanical ingredients have limited authorised claims and possible Novel Food issues [R§4]. Formulation needs regulatory review before launch.
4. **Women's bundle** (cycle/menopause), as a subscription, for the secondary segment [R§4].

Kids' products are **excluded**: Stiftung Warentest called all 18 tested kids' products "superfluous" [R§3], so the reputation risk is high.

## 4. Go-to-Market Plan (GTM)

The channel order follows the report's recommended sequence: D2C + Amazon → drugstore → pharmacy only for clinically supported products [R§4, Opportunity 6].

### Phase 0: Compliance foundation (before launch, est. 3–6 months)
- BVL notification for **every product** (§5 NemV, free, online) [R§4].
- German labels to §4 NemV + LMIV: sales name, % NRV, mandatory warnings; mandatory information shown before purchase in the online shop [R§4].
- Claims review of all packaging, website and influencer briefing texts against Reg. 432/2012, ideally with a food-law firm, because Abmahnungen are used actively [R§4].
- Packaging registration (VerpackG / LUCID) [R§4].
- Set up the batch-testing process with an accredited lab and the QR-code certificate page.
- Duration, lab costs and contract manufacturer choice: **insufficient information, further research recommended.**

### Phase 1: D2C launch + content (months 1–6)
- **Channel:** own online shop with subscription model (recurring revenue) [R§4].
- **Content marketing as the main driver, not paid social.** The report expects high CAC in D2C (estimate) and cites foodspring's exit as a warning [R§4]. Content ideas based on the report's facts:
  - "Dose check": an explainer series on BfR maximum levels and why "more" is not better.
  - Plain-language guides correcting common myths (for example "supplements are not safety-tested before sale"; "German maximum levels are not legally binding") [R§3].
  - Publishing our own batch results, including the process.
- **Earned media / PR:** use test publication cycles (Öko-Test, Stiftung Warentest) to position KLARDOSIS as the brand built to pass these tests. Actively send products for testing once the range is stable. A good result is the strongest trust signal; a poor one is the biggest risk [R§4, Barrier 2].
- **Influencers:** only a small number, with written briefs, pre-approved claims and contractual compliance clauses, because about 90% of influencer statements on supplements were found not permissible [R§4].
- **KPIs to track:** repeat purchase / subscription rate, CAC, share of traffic from organic content, review score. Target values: **insufficient information** (no benchmarks in the report).

### Phase 2: Amazon (from about month 3–4)
- Amazon is a major D2C channel (e.g. Natural Elements) where reviews and rankings decide [R§4].
- Goal: collect reviews and reach buyers who do not find our shop. Show the batch QR code and the "Dosed within BfR guidance" mark in listings.
- Risk: intense price pressure [R§4]. Amazon's share of German supplement sales is unknown [R§5]: **insufficient information, further research recommended** before setting channel budgets.

### Phase 3: Drugstore listing (from about months 12–18, based on proven demand)
- Target: dm, Rossmann, Müller, the biggest volume channel [R§4]. They list strong D2C brands but require proof of demand [R§4].
- Pitch to buyers: "the transparent premium alternative" next to their own private labels, which received negative test results. This also gives the secondary segment (mainstream women 30–60) access to us where they already shop.
- Requirements for listing (sales thresholds, terms, margins): **insufficient information, further research recommended.**

### Phase 4: Extensions (from about month 18+, optional)
- **Fitness range** for 18–34 (protein, creatine) with Kölner Liste testing and compliant influencer marketing, as the "legally solid alternative" to TQG [R§4, Opportunities 3 and 5].
- **Pharmacy channel** only for products with clinical support, because entry needs a wholesaler, PZN and field force [R§4].
- **Sustainability upgrades** such as refill or glass packaging [R§4]. The priority depends on survey data that is currently missing [R§5].

### Key risks and how we handle them
| Risk [R§4] | Response |
|---|---|
| Poor test rating | Formulate to BfR values; test every batch; no overdosing by design. |
| Abmahnung on claims | Only Union-list claims; legal review of all content, including influencers. |
| High CAC in D2C | Content and PR first; subscriptions to raise customer lifetime value; Amazon as a second acquisition channel. |
| EU maximum levels (~2028) | Already below BfR values; monitor the Commission draft (publication status unverified [R§5]). |
| Sunday Natural copies the transparency position | Move fast and own the "correct dose" message. Whether they already do it: insufficient information. |
